Maximalist Decor Ideas (and How Eclectic Decor Fits In)

Maximalist decor ideas that feel collected, not cluttered: how to pick an anchor piece, build a palette, mix patterns, and where eclectic decor fits in.

Erynn

October 8, 2026

Maximalist decor has a reputation for being messy. The best maximalist rooms are anything but. They are full, yes, but every layer has a reason to be there.

Here is how to get the rich, collected look without the chaos, plus where eclectic decor fits in.

Key takeaways

  • Maximalism is intentional abundance, not just more stuff.
  • Start with one anchor piece and pull your palette from it.
  • Mix patterns at different scales and group your collections.
  • Eclectic decor mixes eras and styles; maximalism turns up the volume.

What Is Maximalist Decor?

Maximalism is the opposite of a pared back, all white room. It celebrates color, pattern, texture, art, and the things you love to collect.

The difference between a great maximalist room and a cluttered one is editing. Everything connects back to a palette, and the room still has a focal point.

Maximalist vs Eclectic Decor

The two get mixed up a lot, and they overlap. A quick way to tell them apart:

Maximalist decorEclectic decor
Big ideaMore color, pattern, and objectsA mix of eras, styles, and sources
VolumeTurned upCan be loud or quiet
Holds it togetherA repeated paletteA shared color, material, or shape
ExamplePatterned walls, full gallery wall, layered rugsMidcentury chair, antique table, modern art

Many of the happiest rooms are both: an eclectic mix of finds, layered with maximalist confidence.

1. Start With an Anchor Piece

Every maximalist room needs a hero. It might be a statement sofa, a bold rug, wallpaper, or a large piece of art.

Buy the anchor first and let everything else respond to it. A piece like the hot pink M&M cookie gives you a clear starting point: hot pink, chocolate brown, and candy brights.

2. Build a Palette of Three to Five Colors

Pull colors straight from your anchor and repeat each one in at least three places. A pink from the art might show up again in a cushion, a lampshade, and a book spine.

Repetition is what makes a busy room feel planned. If you are not sure where to start, our dopamine decor ideas include a few palettes that work.

3. Mix Patterns at Different Scales

Pair one large pattern, one medium, and one small. A big floral, a medium stripe, and a tiny check can live together happily if they share at least one color.

Three large patterns at the same scale will fight. Vary the size and the room relaxes.

4. Layer Texture

Texture is what makes maximalism feel rich instead of flat. Mix velvet, rattan, glossy ceramics, brass, and wood.

Dimensional art helps here too. A sculpted piece reads differently from a framed print, which keeps a full gallery wall from feeling like a single flat surface.

5. Go Big on the Walls

Walls are where maximalist decor really shines. Some ideas:

  • A floor to ceiling gallery wall mixing paintings, prints, plates, and mirrors
  • Bold wallpaper on one wall, with art layered right on top
  • One piece of sculptural decor in the middle of a gallery to give it depth
  • Picture ledges you can restyle whenever you get bored

Lightweight pieces make gallery walls much easier. Most of our cookies weigh 2 to 4 lbs and have a built in hanger, so they slot in next to framed art on a single nail.

6. Group Your Collections

Scattered objects look like clutter. The same objects grouped together look like a collection.

Put like with like: all the vintage glass on one shelf, all the candy colored ceramics on another. Odd numbers and varied heights keep groups interesting.

7. Do Not Forget the Kitchen

Maximalism is not just for living rooms. Open shelves full of colorful dishes, a patterned runner, and fun kitchen wall art bring the style into the room you use most.

A pair works especially well over a banquette or table. The M&M cookie set is two 16 inch cookies made to hang side by side.

8. Edit, Then Edit Again

Every few months, take one thing out. If you do not miss it, it can live somewhere else. Maximalism should feel abundant, never crowded.

Quick check: stand in the doorway. If your eye lands on the anchor first, the room is working. If it bounces around with nowhere to land, remove a few small things.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is maximalist decor?

Maximalist decor is a more is more style built on layers of color, pattern, texture, and personal collections. Done well, it feels rich and collected rather than cluttered, because every layer connects back to a clear palette and an anchor piece.

What is the difference between maximalist and eclectic decor?

Maximalism is about quantity and intensity: lots of color, pattern, and objects. Eclectic decor is about mixing: different eras, styles, and sources in one room. A room can be both, but an eclectic room can also be fairly calm, and a maximalist room can stick to a single style.

How do I make maximalism look intentional?

Start with one anchor piece, pull three to five colors from it, and repeat those colors around the room. Mix patterns at different scales, group collections together instead of scattering them, and leave a few calm spots for the eye to rest.

Does maximalist decor work in a small space?

Yes. Use the walls, since vertical space is free. A gallery wall, bold wallpaper on one wall, and one sculptural piece of art add a lot of personality without taking up floor space.
